| D2 | Token meaning (v1) | The holder's right to receive **principal + a predefined return** (at the **investor rate**, D26) under the investment contract, at a **predefined maturity**. In v1 that right is **against the fund**, which issues every token (D14). |
| D3 | Legal / role split | **The fund assesses and approves projects and is the issuer of the tokens** under its licence. **The platform (our company) carries out the tokenisation** on the fund's instruction: configuring, deploying and minting tokens and operating the registry. | D6 | Blockchain | **Polygon PoS**. Keys stay with us (providers only relay signed transactions); every write is confirmed on two endpoints. Our own node is optional and plugs in as one more endpoint if providers block us or before a broad launch. Which endpoints are reachable from Iran can change, so the list keeps endpoints on several different domains. |
| D7 | Secondary market | Not in v1; switched on per series later. |
| D8 | UI | Mobile-first, responsive web, Farsi, RTL, Jalali calendar. |
| D9 | Money flow | Deposits only from the organisation's own verified bank account. Money stays in the platform balance until committed; on cancellation it returns to the balance and can be withdrawn to the org's own verified account. Raised money is disbursed to the **project company**; the project company repays the **fund**; the fund keeps its spread (D26, D27) and pays investors at maturity. |
| D10 | Issuance policy | Only initiatives that have already started, with a guaranteed return. |
| D11 | Return model (v1) | Simple return only. Rates can be quoted **annually** (Jalali day-count, D19) or **monthly** (whole Jalali months: return = face × monthly rate × months; maturity falls on the same Jalali day as issue). Compound later. |
| D12 | Under-subscription | Per series: `all_or_nothing` or `keep_raised` (optional minimum). |
| D13 | Organisations | Several representatives per organisation, with roles and task assignment. |
| D14 | Issuer (v1) | **Only the fund issues tokens.** Companies (later possibly individuals) are **applicants / project companies**: they request financing for a project, the fund assesses it, and after approval the fund issues the series for that project and the platform tokenises it. Projects the fund assessed before the platform existed: see D23. Other issuers may be allowed in a later phase. Enforced in the database: exactly one org is the fund, and only it can be an issuer. |
| D15 | Guarantees | Required guarantees **depend on the investment type and the outcome of the credit assessment**. A configurable policy matrix (investment type × credit grade → required guarantee types and coverage). Guarantees are given by the project company (or its guarantors) to the fund. Coverage is measured against what the **applicant** owes (principal + applicant-rate return). |
| D16 | Investor visibility | **The fund** sees every series' investors (organisation name, ID and amount). **Project companies see only the amount raised** for their project, not who invested. |
| D17 | Single-user organisations | Allowed. In single-user mode, money actions need an OTP step-up confirmation instead of a second person. A new org starts in single-user mode; it ends automatically when a second member joins and cannot be switched back on while there are two or more. |
| D18 | Spec language | Specs and code in **English**; the product UI is Farsi. |
| D19 | v1 defaults (confirmed) | **Return:** each day weighted by the length of its Jalali year (365/366); issue date included, maturity date excluded; rounded down to the rial per unit. **Subscriptions:** no global cap (min/max per series); first come, first served when over-subscribed; investors may cancel during the offer (per-series flag). **Other:** no tax withholding in v1; no return on idle balances; committee default 3 members, quorum 3, simple majority (configurable). |
| D20 | Service provider: Jibit | **Inquiry:** Shahkar, legal identity with signatories, board and CEO, IBAN ↔ legal entity match, postal code, bounced cheques. **Cobank** on the fund's collection account: one payment ID (شناسه پرداخت) per organisation and per repayment; deposits verified with Jibit's own check that the source account matches the payer's national ID (KYT), with verify / hold / refund; statement and balance; outgoing transfers (internal/Paya/Satna) direct from the collection account. **SMS:** OTP + templates. **Digital contracts:** financing contract + series terms. **Direct debit** (P1) for repayments. **Card gateway** optional (P2). Details: `04-integrations-jibit.md` v0.2. |
| D21 | Key contracts | Two contracts per project, **signed digitally via Jibit** (video KYC + signing certificate): (1) the **financing contract** between the project company and the fund (amount, **applicant rate**, repayment, guarantees, service fees); (2) the **series terms** issued by the fund to investors (**investor rate**). Investor subscriptions use OTP acceptance by default (digital signature above a threshold, P1). |
| D22 | Batch on-chain ops | `SeriesToken.mintBatch` / `burnBatch` (≤200 holders per tx, about 6.3M gas for 200) for issuance and redemption. |
| D23 | Pre-assessed projects | Projects the fund has **already credit-scored, reviewed and approved outside the platform**, which it now wants to offer online. They **skip the on-platform assessment** (screening, credit scoring, due diligence, committee) and enter at the **terms** stage. The fund is the issuer of record (D14). **A fund staff member authorised by the fund manager uploads** the evidence: credit grade, approval date and reference (e.g. committee minutes), the reviewed documents, and the guarantees already held; the fund manager confirms the import. **Valid for 12 months** from the earlier approval date; older assessments go through the normal pipeline. Imported guarantees are **not re-checked**. Every such series is **labelled "assessed by the fund before onboarding"** with the assessment date: in the records and audit log, on the offering page, in the series terms and in the investor's consent. |
| D24 | Withdrawals | To the org's own verified bank accounts only, after a 24 h cooling-off on new accounts. Org maker–checker (or single-user step-up) → finance officer → **fund manager too at or above a threshold** (default 10 billion rials; never the same person as finance) → finance sends via Cobank after checking the collection account's daily/monthly limit. Off in production until tested in Jibit's sandbox. |
| D25 | Service fees | **The applicant company pays for the assessment services** (e.g. credit scoring, due diligence, guarantee review). The fund manager keeps a **price list** in the fund panel: per service a price (rials), the fund's share (the platform gets the rest) and when it is charged; every change is a new version with before/after in the audit log. Applicants can see the list before applying. **When screening passes, an invoice is issued from the active list** and the application waits in `awaiting_payment`; credit assessment starts only when the invoice is paid (from the company's balance, topped up by its payment ID) or **waived by the fund manager with a reason**. Withdrawing or expiring the application cancels an unpaid invoice. With an empty price list, screening goes straight to credit assessment. Pilot example: 20,000,000 toman in total. |
| D26 | Two rates and the spread | Every series has an **applicant rate** (what the project company pays) and an **investor rate** (what investors earn); **investor rate ≤ applicant rate − minimum spread**. The difference is **the fund's income**. Defaults (rate basis, both rates, minimum spread) are a versioned **rate policy** set by the fund manager; each series can override them at the terms stage. Both rates are in the frozen terms and the `termsHash`. Pilot example: applicant 5.6% a month, investors 4% a month, 12 months → applicant pays 67.2%, investors get 48%, **the fund keeps 19.2% of principal** (3.84 billion rials on a 20 billion rial series). |
| D27 | Repayment order | At maturity the collected repayment is split by a **waterfall** set in the rate policy. Default **`fund_first`**: the fund's spread first, then investors' principal + return. `investors_first` and `pro_rata` are also available. On a full repayment the order makes no difference; on a shortfall it decides who carries it (see §4). |

## 3. Blockchain stack (summary)
- Polygon PoS mainnet, Amoy testnet. RPC through several providers with request-level failover (timeouts, 5xx, rate limits move to the next endpoint; contract reverts are returned as the chain's answer) and two-endpoint confirmation, so no single provider can stop us (sanctions and IP-blocking risk). Own node optional later: 16 cores, 64 GB RAM, 6 TB NVMe, 1 Gbit/s (Polygon's recommended full-node spec).
- OpenZeppelin 5.6 contracts: `IdentityRegistry`, `SeriesFactory`, `SeriesToken` (ERC-20, 0 decimals, allowlist, transfers locked, `termsHash`, batch mint/burn, freeze, forcedTransfer, pause).
- **Amoy testnet deployment (2026-11-08):** IdentityRegistry `0x7d72E78c129B7BF82Ef78F9327Daf5c3E279fbDC`, SeriesFactory `0x0a2e368D6132aD43aB0e6b5F10c66B316112FeF4`. Testnet mode (one key holds admin + operator; that key was exposed in chat, so testnet only). Measured on Amoy: deployment ≈3.2M gas; one series lifecycle (3 registrations, create, mint, freeze/unfreeze, burn) 2.15M gas ≈ 0.064 POL.
- **Chain outbox:** business code never calls the chain. It writes a `chain_txs` row in the same DB transaction (KYB approval, KYB expiry, re-KYB, fund designation); the worker sends it, backs off on failure, and checks the registry before sending so nothing is sent twice. The digital ID follows the database: registered and up to date while KYB is valid, revoked when it expires or is rejected, reinstated and updated on re-approval. Flags: every org = investor; the fund org also = issuer + fund (D14).
- Operator hot key (pays gas, registers identities, mints and burns) through Web3Signer + OpenBao in production. Admin role on a 2-of-3 Safe multisig.
- No personal data on-chain, only hashes (`orgIdHash` = keccak of salted org ID, `kybHash` = keccak of the approved KYB case).
- Compiled with solc-js 0.8.24 from npm (the compiler download host is not reachable from some networks).

## 4. Legal-track items (deferred, but tracked)
- Islamic contract wrapper for a guaranteed fixed return.
- Basis for the platform's tokenisation authority alongside the fund's licence.
- Holding client money in the fund's collection account, operated through Jibit Cobank.
- Legal validity of Jibit digital signatures for company signatories.
- POL purchase and holding under Central Bank crypto rules.
- Privacy and consent for sharing investor identities (with the fund only).
- Tax on returns.
- Use of foreign infrastructure providers (RPC providers such as dRPC) from our legal setup and hosting location, and how to pay them.
- Offering pre-assessed projects (D23): what investors must be told about an assessment done before onboarding (its date and scope).
- The fund as issuer of every series (D14): the fund carries the obligation to investors even if a project company repays late; how this sits with the fund's licence and capital.
- **Rates and spread (D26):** whether the applicant rate (e.g. 5.6% a month ≈ 67% a year, simple) is within the limits that apply to the fund's financing, and how the spread is described in the contracts (a fee for arranging and guaranteeing, or a margin).
- **Fund-first repayment (D27):** if the fund takes its spread before investors on a shortfall, investors carry more of the loss; this must be disclosed in the series terms and risk statement, and checked against the fund's obligation as issuer (D14).
- **VAT on service fees (D25):** whether the assessment services are subject to VAT (the policy has a VAT rate, default 0).
